Gudauri or Bakuriani: where should a beginner go

Updated: 23 November 2026 · 6 min read

Both resorts suit beginners. Gudauri is closer to Tbilisi, higher and has a bigger ski area, and it’s easier to find snow there all season. Bakuriani is further, lower and calmer, with a reputation as a cosy family resort with gentle slopes. The choice depends on what matters more to you: snow and a variety of runs, or a relaxed holiday. Below is a point-by-point comparison with sources.

We work in Gudauri, so we know it better and won’t pretend to know both resorts equally well. About Bakuriani we only write what we could verify in official and reliable sources.

Comparison for beginners

Gudauri Bakuriani
Distance from Tbilisi about 120 km about 180 km

Top altitude up to 3,276 m up to 2,702 m

Lift hours 10:00–17:00 10:00–17:00

Sources: the resort pages on the operator MTA’s website — Gudauri and Bakuriani; the National Tourism Administration — Gudauri and Bakuriani; the Bakuriani ski pass price — according to SOVA, November 2026; the Bakuriani top altitude — skiresort.com. We left run lengths out of the table: different sources give very different figures for both resorts.

Getting there

Gudauri is closer: about 120 km along the Georgian Military Road, 2–2.5 hours on average. The downside is the mountain hairpins and traffic restrictions after heavy snowfall. Details in “How to get from Tbilisi to Gudauri”.

Bakuriani is further: about 180 km, via Borjomi. The famous Borjomi — Bakuriani narrow-gauge railway (the “Kukushka”) hasn’t run since 2020. The economy minister promised to resume service in December 2026, but no timetable or prices have been announced yet. If you want to ride the Kukushka, check closer to your trip.

Runs and snow

Gudauri is higher: the top is at about 3,276 m, and the resort has more than 20 lifts according to the operator. For a beginner this means room to grow: you start in the practice area and after a few days move on to harder runs without changing resorts. How the first day works is in “Gudauri for beginners”.

Bakuriani is lower: the top is at about 2,702 m. The operator runs three areas — Didveli, Kokhta and Mitarbi — and one ski pass is valid in all three. The Tourism Administration describes the Didveli runs as “gentler, for beginners and families”.

Atmosphere

Gudauri is a resort people go to first and foremost to ski, including freeriders: besides the pistes, there are opportunities for off-piste riding with a guide. More in “Freeriding in Gudauri”.

Bakuriani is a resort village at about 1,700 m, with a reputation as a calm family place. If atmosphere and walks matter to you as much as skiing, that’s a strong argument. Borjomi is nearby, so it’s easy to combine the trip with a stay in the spa town.

Kids

Both resorts are good for children. The same operator rules apply to the lifts at both: children under 6 ride for free, ages 6–12 pay the child rate.

  • For Gudauri: it’s closer and quicker to reach, has a practice area, and the season runs until April according to the operator.
  • For Bakuriani: the gentle Didveli runs and a calm atmosphere.

We teach children from age 3 in Gudauri. How to prepare is in “Gudauri with kids”.

Which suits whom

Choose Gudauri if:

  • you have only a few days and don’t want a long drive;
  • reliable snow and a variety of runs matter to you;
  • you want to go from your first lesson to skiing on your own within one trip;
  • you’re interested in freeriding, snowmobiles or paragliding.

Choose Bakuriani if:

  • a calm family holiday and walks matter more to you;
  • you don’t mind a longer drive;
  • you plan to combine skiing with Borjomi.

FAQ

Which has more runs?

Different sources give very different figures for run length, so we don’t recommend comparing on that basis. According to the operator, Gudauri has 21 lifts and Bakuriani 12, and Gudauri sits higher.

Where is the ski pass cheaper?

In the 2026/27 season a day pass for foreigners costs 120 lari in Gudauri and, according to SOVA, 100 lari in Bakuriani.

Can I ski both resorts on one pass?

The season MultiSki is valid at all four of the operator’s resorts, including Gudauri and Bakuriani. For shorter passes, check the conditions at the ticket office.

Where is it better to learn from zero?

Both resorts have beginner areas. We teach in Gudauri: we start in the practice area and move on to the runs when you’re ready. If you’re going to Gudauri, message us and we’ll find you a time.

Where should we go with young children?

Both work. For young children the journey is often decisive: Gudauri is about two hours away, Bakuriani 2.5–3.5 hours. Children under 6 ride the lifts for free at both resorts. We teach children from age 3 in Gudauri — tell us your child’s age and we’ll suggest how to structure the first lesson.

Can I learn in one place and then go to the other?

Of course. The basic skills — stance, stopping, turning — are the same at any resort. Many people learn in Gudauri first and go to Bakuriani the following year, or the other way round. The main thing is that your first lessons are with an instructor and you leave wanting to come back.
