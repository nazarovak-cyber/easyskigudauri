What to bring for your first day on the slopes: a checklist

For your first day on the slopes you need warm, waterproof clothing in layers, gloves, goggles, sunscreen and ID. Skis or a snowboard, boots and a helmet are easier to rent. Below is a detailed list with explanations, and at the end a short version you can print or save on your phone.

Dress in layers

In the mountains the weather changes within an hour: it’s hot in the sun and cold on the lift in the wind. So you don’t just dress “warmer” — you dress in layers.

Base layer — thermal underwear

Its job is to wick moisture away from your body. Synthetic fabric or merino wool works. Cotton doesn’t: a T-shirt gets wet and keeps you cold all day.

Mid layer — insulation

A fleece, a thin wool sweater or a light down vest. On a warm spring day you can put the mid layer in your backpack. If you get cold easily, take two thin mid layers instead of one thick one: that makes it easier to adjust to the weather during the day.

Outer layer — protection from snow and wind

A ski jacket and pants with a membrane: windproof and waterproof. If you don’t have your own, you can rent them — how much that costs is in “Ski rental in Gudauri”.

Socks

One pair of ski socks. Two pairs or thick wool socks in a boot are a common mistake: your foot sweats, gets cold and rubs.

Small things people forget

These little things can cut your day on the slopes short:

  • High-SPF sunscreen. In the mountains you get sunburnt even in cloudy weather, especially your face below the goggles.
  • Lip balm — lips crack quickly in the wind.
  • Goggles. Ordinary sunglasses don’t help at speed or in snowfall.
  • Gloves or mittens. Beginners touch the snow with their hands a lot. If you have a spare pair, bring it.
  • A neck gaiter or balaclava — to cover your face from the wind on the lift.
  • A thin hat — under the helmet if your ears get cold.
  • A small backpack — if you ski with one, make sure it sits snugly on your back.
  • Water and a snack — skiing takes a lot of energy.
  • Cash in lari — in case somewhere doesn’t take cards. Last season the ski pass offices accepted both cash and cards.
  • ID. For a teen ski pass you need to show an original document at the ticket office, under the operator’s rules.
  • Your phone in a warm pocket. In the cold the battery drains fast.

What not to bring

  • Jeans and cotton hoodies — they get wet.
  • A scarf — it can catch on the lift. A neck gaiter is better.
  • Jewellery and expensive watches — easy to lose in the snow.

If you’re travelling with a child

Children need all the same things, plus spare mittens, a snack and warm tea in a thermos. It’s important that a child doesn’t get cold in the first half hour — otherwise the lesson won’t go well. More in “Gudauri with kids”.

Before you leave the hotel

  1. Check the weather and webcams — especially the wind.
  2. See which lifts are open in the Mountain Live section.
  3. Put on sunscreen in advance, not on the slope.
  4. If you have a lesson, message your instructor that you’re on your way.

Printable checklist

Clothing: thermal underwear · fleece · ski jacket · ski pants · one pair of ski socks · thin hat · neck gaiter.

Protection: gloves or mittens (+ spare pair) · goggles · helmet (rental) · sunscreen · lip balm.

Gear (rental): skis with poles or a snowboard · boots.

Take with you: ID · cash in lari and a card · phone · water · snack.

FAQ

Can I ski in sunglasses instead of goggles?

On a calm sunny day you can, but goggles are more reliable: they protect your eyes from wind, snow and glare and don’t fly off when you fall. Beginners are better off with goggles.

Do I need a backpack for the lesson?

No. Your pockets are enough for the lesson: phone, sunscreen, lip balm. You don’t need to take photos of yourself either — the instructor films you during the lesson and sends you the photos and videos in a messenger.

What if my gloves get wet?

Swap them for your spare pair. That’s why we recommend bringing a second pair: wet gloves quickly turn icy in the cold.

Should I bring cash?

Yes, it’s worth having some cash in lari in case somewhere doesn’t take cards. Last season the ski pass offices accepted both cash and Visa and Mastercard cards.

How should I dress if the forecast is warm?

Still in layers: in the morning on the lift and in the wind it can be cold, and by lunchtime it’s hot. It’s easier to take off a layer and put it in your pocket than to freeze.

Can I rent clothing?

Yes, jackets and pants are often available to rent — according to public data, about 20–25 lari a day for each item. But you need your own thermal underwear, socks and gloves.

What should I bring if my lesson is in the morning and I want to ski all day?

Bring what you’ll need after the lesson: water, a snack, sunscreen for reapplying and spare gloves. A small backpack or roomy pockets will do the job. You can buy a ski pass for the rest of the day after the lesson — ask us whether it makes sense to buy a full-day pass straight away.
