Ski rental in Gudauri: what to rent and how much it costs

In Gudauri a beginner only needs to rent skis with poles and boots, or a snowboard with boots, plus a helmet. That comes to roughly 50–70 lari a day for a set and about 10 lari for a helmet. You can rent clothing too, but it’s more comfortable to come in your own. The most important thing is the boots: they should fit snugly without pinching.

Below: what exactly to rent, how to choose the size and where the prices come from.

What a beginner must rent

There’s no need to buy your own gear before your first lesson. You don’t know yet what suits you, and at a rental shop you can change the size or even swap skis for a board.

The essential set:

  • Skis with poles or a snowboard. If you’re not sure which, read “Skiing or snowboarding: what should a beginner choose”.
  • Boots. Ski boots for skis, soft snowboard boots for a board.
  • A helmet. Everyone falls on their first day — don’t go on the slope without one.

Optional extras:

  • A jacket and pants, if you don’t have waterproof clothing.
  • Goggles — some shops rent them, but it’s better to bring your own since you wear them on your face.

How to choose the size

Boots

Boots are the most important part of your rental. In uncomfortable boots, a lesson turns into torture.

  • Wear one thin ski sock — not two pairs and not thick wool.
  • The boot should hold your heel firmly: when you lean forward, your foot shouldn’t lift.
  • Your toes may lightly touch the front when you stand straight and move back when you bend your knees.
  • Don’t go a size up “for comfort”: your foot will slide around and the ski will be harder to control.
  • Walk around in the boots for 5–10 minutes right in the shop. If something pinches now, it will hurt in an hour.

Skis and board

Ski and board length for a beginner depends on height, weight and level — the rental staff will choose it for you. Tell them honestly that it’s your first time: beginners get shorter, softer skis that are easier to turn.

Helmet

A helmet should sit snugly and not slip over your eyes when you shake your head. Fasten the strap: you should be able to fit one finger between it and your chin. A loose helmet shifts in a fall and protects you less. If you wear goggles, try them on together: there shouldn’t be a gap on your forehead between the helmet and the goggles.

Should you bring your own clothing

Yes, if you have it. Your own clothes are more familiar and cleaner, and you won’t waste time trying things on. Any waterproof, windproof jacket and pants will do, with thermal underwear and a fleece underneath.

What not to do:

  • ski in jeans — they get wet and make you cold;
  • wear a down jacket without a membrane — it quickly gets soaked by snow;
  • forget your gloves — your own warm waterproof ones are the most reliable.

FAQ

Do I need to book rentals in advance?

In high season and during holidays there can be queues, and your size may not be available. If you’re travelling on those dates, it’s better to arrange things in advance — we’ll tell you where to go.

What if my boots rub?

Don’t put up with it. Go back to the shop and ask for a different model or size. A blister in the first hour will ruin both the lesson and the next day.

Should I rent goggles?

If you can, bring your own: you wear them on your face, and they need to fit your helmet. If you don’t have any, ask at the shop.

Can I swap skis for a snowboard?

Usually yes — check the exchange terms at your shop. It’s handy if you want to try both.

Will you help with rentals?

Yes. Before your lesson, tell us where you’re staying and we’ll suggest a trusted shop nearby; during the lesson the instructor will check your boots and adjust the bindings.

Do I need protective gear for snowboarding?

It really helps a beginner on a snowboard: in the first days you fall more often than on skis, mostly onto your knees and back. If the shop has knee pads and padded shorts, take them. Wrist guards are a good idea too.

How can I tell the gear is in good shape?

Look at the skis or board: the base shouldn’t have deep scratches down to the core, and the edges should be free of rust and chips. The boots should buckle without effort and hold your foot firmly. If anything looks off, ask for a replacement straight away.
