Gudauri for beginners: where to ski on your first day

Yes, Gudauri is good for beginners: the resort has a separate practice area with a “magic carpet” lift and gentle runs for your first descents. That’s where you should start, not on the upper slopes. The first two hours are best spent with an instructor: they’ll set up your basic stance and stopping, and by the end of the lesson you’ll ride down on your own.

Below: where to ski on your first day, how that day goes and which mistakes to avoid.

Is Gudauri good for beginners

Gudauri is a big resort with runs of every difficulty, from practice areas to steep slopes at the top. That’s good for a beginner: you can start at the bottom and within a few days move on to harder runs without changing resorts.

We’ve been teaching here for more than 11 years, over 1,000 students have learned with us, and most of them had never stood on skis or a board before. So we can say from experience: you can learn to ski in Gudauri, and it’s neither scary nor slow. The key is to start the right way.

Practice slopes and first runs

On the operator MTA’s resort page, beginner runs are marked: Pirveli and Snow Park. The same page lists the Beginners Ski Area with a “magic carpet” — a moving belt you ride up standing, with no chair or drag lift.

For the 2026/27 season the operator promises new additions, including a 200-metre magic carpet, two new lifts and two new runs. Which of them will open and when will become clear by the start of the season — check the Mountain Live section.

What a beginner should know:

  • Don’t go up to the top on your first day. The upper runs are longer and steeper — go there once you can stop and turn confidently.
  • The practice area is normal, not embarrassing. Everyone skis there, adults and kids alike.
  • Runs are graded by difficulty. Start with the ones marked for beginners on the resort map.

How your first day goes

If you learn with us, the lesson lasts 2 hours and goes step by step:

  1. Getting to know you and your gear. We assess your level, pick skis or a snowboard and adjust the bindings for you.
  2. Balance on flat ground. We learn to stand, glide and stop where falling isn’t scary.
  3. First descents. Short, safe slopes, with the instructor right next to you every metre.
  4. Riding down on your own. We reinforce the technique — and you ride down by yourself, without panic.

In the first lesson people usually master the stance, stopping and speed control. How fast depends on your age, fitness and format: private or a mini-group of up to three people.

During the lesson the instructor films you and afterwards sends you the photos and videos in a messenger. It’s a gift, and it’s handy for reviewing your mistakes later.

What to do before the lesson

  • Sort out your rentals in advance, or let us help you choose. Details in “Ski rental in Gudauri”.
  • Check whether you need a ski pass on that day. We explain it in “Gudauri ski pass”.
  • Dress in layers and bring goggles and gloves. The list is here.

Why the first 2 hours are best with an instructor

You can teach yourself, but it’s usually slow, painful and leaves you with bad habits that are hard to fix later. The first two hours decide whether you’ll enjoy skiing.

What an instructor gives you right at the start:

  • The right stance from the first minutes. One of the most common reasons beginners fall is that their weight “sits” back.
  • Stopping before the first descent. Once you know how to stop, you stop being afraid of the slope.
  • A safe spot. The instructor knows where to learn calmly and where it’s too early for a beginner.
  • Lifts without stress. Getting on a lift for the first time scares many people — the instructor will show you how it’s done.
  • Attention on you. Our groups have no more than three people, or the lesson is fully private.

Common beginner mistakes

  1. Going up to the top “for the view”. Getting down from there is hard and scary for a beginner.
  2. Learning from a friend who skis well. Skiing and teaching are different skills, and arguments on the slope ruin a holiday.
  3. Skiing until you drop on day one. Fatigue is a common cause of injuries. Better to stop while you still want more.
  4. Skimping on a helmet and goggles. A helmet is cheap to rent, and everyone falls on their first day.
  5. Picking a day with strong wind. Check the forecast — how to do it is described in “Gudauri weather”.
  6. Spending ages choosing between skis and a board. If you’re unsure, read “Skiing or snowboarding: what should a beginner choose” — and just start.

FAQ

How many lessons do I need to start skiing on my own?

You make your first solo descent in your very first lesson. How many more lessons you’ll need to ride the runs confidently depends on your age, fitness and how much you ski between lessons. We usually discuss this at the end of the first lesson, once we can see how it’s going.

Is it too late to learn as an adult?

No. Many of our students start as adults. Everyone learns at their own pace, so we adapt the lesson to the person, not to a schedule.

Is getting on the lift scary?

A little scary for almost everyone. At first you don’t need a lift at all: you learn on flat ground and on the magic carpet. When the time comes, the instructor will show you how to get on and off and will be right next to you.

What if I can’t do it?

You will — just at your own pace. We teach privately or in mini-groups of up to three people, so the instructor sees your mistakes specifically and doesn’t rush you.

Where should a beginner stay?

It’s most convenient to stay close to the practice area so you don’t waste energy walking to the slope in ski boots. If you’ve already booked accommodation, tell us where it is and we’ll suggest the best place to meet for your lesson and the nearest rental shop.
