How to get from Tbilisi to Gudauri

Updated: 23 November 2026 · 6 min read

Gudauri is about 120 kilometres from Tbilisi along the Georgian Military Road. By car the drive takes about 2–2.5 hours, sometimes longer in winter. There are four ways to get there: by marshrutka (minibus) from Didube bus station, by shared taxi from the same place, by pre-booked transfer or in your own car. In winter, always check before you set off that the road isn’t closed.

How long the journey takes

The Georgian National Tourism Administration gives a distance of about 120 km and a travel time of about two hours. In practice, allow more:

  • 2–2.5 hours — on average, according to the Wander-Lush travel guide (May 2026);
  • 3.5 hours or more — in winter, during snowfall and at weekends, according to gudauri.com (September 2026).

The road climbs up a series of hairpin bends, and the last kilometres before the resort are a mountain section. If you get carsick, sit in the front and take a pill in advance.

Options: marshrutka, taxi, transfer, own car

There’s no official timetable or fare for this route. The figures below come from the Wander-Lush guide (May 2026) and other open sources. Check the price with the driver before the trip.

Option Cost Travel time Notes
Marshrutka from Didube bus station about 15 lari per person, cash to the driver 2.5–3 hours leaves when full
Shared taxi from Didube 40–50 lari per person about 2–2.5 hours passengers are gathered on the spot
Transfer via GoTrip from 180 lari per car about 2–2.5 hours book in advance, private ride
Regular taxi 300–350 lari per car, according to bloggers about 2–2.5 hours more expensive than a transfer
Own or rental car fuel and rental from 2 hours requires confidence on mountain roads

Marshrutka

The cheapest option. Marshrutkas to Gudauri and to Kazbegi (Stepantsminda) leave from Didube bus station in Tbilisi. The Kazbegi marshrutka also passes through Gudauri — tell the driver where to drop you off. The downside: carrying skis and luggage in a minibus is awkward, and it only leaves once it has enough passengers.

Taxi and transfer

If you’re travelling as a family or with a lot of luggage, a transfer costs only a little more than marshrutka tickets for everyone, and you’re picked up at your hotel and taken to the door. Arrange it in advance and check that the price is per car, not per person.

Own car

You can drive yourself, but in winter this is a mountain road with snow and ice. You need winter tyres and experience driving on hairpin bends. We couldn’t find official requirements for snow chains on passenger cars — ask the rental company whether chains are included.

The road in winter and possible closures

After heavy snowfall and when there’s a risk of avalanches, the Georgian Military Road is partly closed. The section most often closed is Gudauri (Post) — Kobi, which is beyond the resort, over the Jvari Pass. Usually passenger cars are let through first, and trucks and trailers later.

Where to check before you set off:

  • Traffic restrictions on the website of the Roads Department of Georgia — updated throughout the day;
  • the department’s hotline: 032 2 31 30 76.

Georgia is building a new Kvesheti — Kobi highway to bypass the most difficult section. According to BM.ge, full opening has been pushed back to 2027. Whether the section to Gudauri is already open is worth checking before your trip.

Travelling with children

  • Choose a transfer, not a marshrutka. Marshrutkas have no child seats and can wait a long time for passengers.
  • Leave in the morning — you’ll arrive and check in while it’s light, even if there are delays on the road.
  • Bring snacks, water and bags — children often get carsick on the hairpins.
  • Don’t plan a lesson on the day you arrive. After the drive a child needs rest; the first lesson is better the next morning.
  • Watch the weather. How to check forecasts and webcams is in “Gudauri weather”.

FAQ

Can I do a day trip from Tbilisi?

Yes, if you leave early. The drive takes about 2–2.5 hours each way, and the lifts run from 10:00 to 17:00. Keep in mind that in winter the road may take longer, and you’ll be driving back in the dark.

How much is a marshrutka to Gudauri?

According to the Wander-Lush guide (May 2026), about 15 lari per person, paid in cash to the driver. There’s no official fare, so check the price before boarding.

Where can I check whether the road is open?

On the restrictions page of the Roads Department of Georgia — georoad.gov.ge — or via its hotline, 032 2 31 30 76.

Is the road closed all the way to Gudauri?

Restrictions usually apply to the Gudauri (Post) — Kobi section, which is beyond the resort. But in heavy snowfall there can be delays on the approach too, so check the status on the day you travel.

Do I need winter tyres?

Yes — driving a mountain road in winter without them is dangerous. If you’re renting a car, ask the rental company about winter tyres and chains.

How do I get there with skis and lots of luggage?

A transfer or taxi is the most convenient: your luggage travels with you and you don’t have to change vehicles. Space for skis and bags in a marshrutka is limited. If you don’t have your own gear, you can rent it right in Gudauri — then the trip is easier.

Does the Kazbegi marshrutka work?

Yes. Marshrutkas to Kazbegi (Stepantsminda) leave from the same Didube station and pass through Gudauri on the way. Tell the driver you need to get off in Gudauri. According to the Wander-Lush guide, this marshrutka may cost up to 20 lari.
