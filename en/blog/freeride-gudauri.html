Freeriding in Gudauri: how to try it safely for the first time

Updated: 23 November 2026 · 6 min read

Contents

  2. Avalanche safety
  3. Why only with a guide
  4. How we find you a guide

You can try freeriding in Gudauri once you ski intermediate runs confidently and can control your speed. Your first trip beyond the pistes should only be with a guide, with avalanche safety gear and after checking the avalanche forecast. Outside the marked runs there’s no avalanche control and no patrol: you and your guide are responsible for your safety there.

What freeriding is and when you’re ready

Freeriding means riding outside groomed runs: in untracked and fresh snow, on slopes that nobody grooms. People have been freeriding in Gudauri for many years, and it has everything you need for a first taste of off-piste, including guides.

You’re ready to try if:

  • you ride intermediate runs confidently anywhere on the slope, not just along the edge;
  • you can stop where you decide to, not where you happen to;
  • you can ski all day without getting exhausted — off-piste tires you out many times faster;
  • you keep your balance well on uneven snow and in chopped-up tracks.

If you’re still learning, it’s too early for freeriding. That’s not a permanent ban: the path from your first lesson to your first off-piste outing usually takes more than one season, and that’s normal. Start with lessons on the piste — you’ll get to the powder faster that way than by heading straight there. Where to start is in “Gudauri for beginners”.

Avalanche safety

The main risk in freeriding isn’t falling — it’s avalanches. Gudauri has periods of increased avalanche danger, and on such days the resort operator asks people to obey the safety signs.

Where to check the avalanche forecast

  • Avalanche Georgia — an independent avalanche forecast for Gudauri on a five-level danger scale. It isn’t the resort’s official service, but it’s the main public bulletin for the region. The last bulletin came out on 20 April 2026; as of 23 November 2026 there were no bulletins for the new season yet.
  • The safety section of the operator’s website — mta.ski/en/safety. It was being updated in November 2026, so check it before the season.

If a run or lift is closed and the entrance is fenced off, the area is closed. Don’t go in, even if someone has already ridden it.

Gear

For freeriding, every member of the group must have:

  • an avalanche transceiver (beacon) — switched on and checked before setting off;
  • a shovel;
  • a probe;
  • a helmet;
  • a backpack that fits all of this;
  • insurance that covers off-piste skiing and helicopter evacuation. Standard travel insurance may not include this — check the terms in advance.

A transceiver, shovel and probe only work together with the skills: you need to know how to use them and how to search for a partner. That’s taught on avalanche courses — ask your guide how to prepare.

Why only with a guide

A freeride guide isn’t there just to show you a beautiful slope. A guide:

  • knows the terrain — where the slope holds and where there’s a drop or a cliff below;
  • reads the avalanche forecast and the snow and decides where it’s OK to go today and where it isn’t;
  • chooses a descent for your level, not theirs;
  • can organise search and rescue if something goes wrong;
  • checks the group’s gear before setting off.

Following strangers’ tracks off-piste, or tagging along with people who ride better than you, is a bad idea: someone else’s track tells you nothing about whether the slope is safe today. Your first outing should only be with a professional guide.

FAQ

Can you freeride on a snowboard?

Yes, freeriding is for both skiers and snowboarders. The requirements are the same: confident riding on the pistes, speed control, avalanche gear and a guide.

How much does a guide cost?

It depends on the format, duration, group size and route. Tell us what you want and what your level is, and we’ll find a guide and give you a price for your request.

Where can I get avalanche gear?

If you don’t have your own, ask us in advance and we’ll tell you where to find it. You must not go beyond the pistes without a transceiver, shovel and probe.

When is the best time to go freeriding?

When the avalanche danger is low and the weather is clear. Check the Avalanche Georgia forecast and the weather forecast before every outing. The final decision on whether to go is made by the guide.

Is freeriding suitable for a beginner?

No. First you need to ride the pistes confidently. If you’re just starting, take a few lessons — we’ll help you get to the powder safely and faster.

Do I need special insurance?

Yes. Choose insurance that explicitly covers off-piste skiing and helicopter evacuation. Many standard policies don’t include this, so read the terms before your trip, not after.

How is freeriding different from skiing on the piste?

On the piste the snow is prepared, the slope has been checked and if something happens a patrol is nearby. Off-piste the snow can be deep, heavy or hard, the terrain unpredictable, and help won’t come right away. That’s why technique, gear and the guide’s decisions matter more here than speed.
