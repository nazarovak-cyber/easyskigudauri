Skiing or snowboarding: what should a beginner choose

Updated: 23 November 2026 · 6 min read

Contents

  2. Which is easier on the first day
  3. What to choose for a child and an adult
  4. Can you try both

In short: skiing is usually easier on the first day, while snowboarding is harder at the start but many people enjoy it more once they’ve got the basics. Choose whichever excites you more — motivation matters more than the difference in difficulty. If you can’t decide, start with the one that feels more natural and try the other on another day.

How they feel different

Skiing. You face forward, your legs move independently and you hold poles. It’s similar to walking and running: your body knows what to do. The feeling is control and speed. Skiing is the classic start for people who want to feel confident on the slope quickly.

Snowboarding. Both feet are strapped to one board and you stand sideways to the slope. At first it feels strange: you can’t step out with a foot to keep your balance. But later you get that special feeling of smooth turns and freedom of movement that people love snowboarding for.

Which is easier on the first day

Skis

  • Easier to stand and move on flat ground.
  • You fall less often, and less painfully.
  • Your first solo descent comes sooner.
  • Learning to turn nicely at speed is harder — but that’s not about day one.

Snowboard

  • More falls on day one, especially onto your back and knees.
  • Harder to get up after a fall and to ride the lift.
  • Protective gear helps: knee pads and padded shorts, if the rental shop offers them.
  • Once it “clicks” and you can turn, progress is often fast.

We don’t promise you’ll master everything in two hours. In the first lesson you’ll learn the basic stance, stopping and speed control, and the result depends on your age, fitness and format. With an instructor, the first day is noticeably easier on both skis and a board.

What to choose for a child and an adult

For a child

Young children usually start on skis: it’s easier to balance and the first result comes sooner. We take children from age 3. Older kids are more likely to try snowboarding once they have enough strength and coordination. If your child is keen on a board — go for it, the desire matters more. More in “Gudauri with kids”.

For an adult

An adult should answer three questions:

  1. What do I like watching? If snowboard videos inspire you, choose the board.
  2. How many days do I have? If only one or two, you’ll probably get further on skis.
  3. Who am I skiing with? If your friends ski and you want to ride together, skis make sense — and vice versa.

If you’ve skateboarded, surfed or wakeboarded, snowboarding will come easier: the stance is similar. If you’ve done cross-country skiing or skating, downhill skiing is often easier.

Can you try both

Yes. Many people do exactly that: a ski lesson one day, a snowboard lesson another. It’s better to feel the difference yourself than to read about it. After two lessons it’s usually clear which suits you: some love the control of skis, others the flow of a board. You can’t go wrong — both give you the same slope and the same views.

How to arrange it:

  • Rent your gear rather than buying it. At a rental shop you can swap skis for a board. What to rent and what it costs is in “Ski rental in Gudauri”.
  • Don’t try both on the same day. After two hours on skis your legs are tired, and snowboarding will be harder. Better to spread them over different days.
  • Start in the practice area either way. Where it is, we explain in “Gudauri for beginners”.

FAQ

Which is easier for an adult to learn?

For most adults the first day on skis goes more smoothly. But if you’re drawn to snowboarding, choose it: with an instructor the early difficulties pass faster, and the desire to learn matters more than the difference in difficulty.

Can you learn in 2 hours?

In the first two-hour lesson you’ll learn the basic stance, stopping and speed control and make your first solo descent. That applies to both skis and a snowboard. After that, progress depends on how much you ride.

How much does ski and snowboard rental cost?

According to recent public data — about 50 lari a day for skis with poles and boots, and about 70 lari for a snowboard with boots. More in the rental article.

Can I switch from skis to a snowboard after the first lesson?

Yes. Rent different gear and book a snowboard lesson for the next day. We teach both.

What should we choose if the whole family is going?

You don’t all have to do the same thing. Often the kids start on skis and one of the adults chooses a board. The main thing is to have lessons at the same time so you can ride together afterwards.

How should I dress for snowboarding?

The same as for skiing — in layers, with one adjustment: on a snowboard you often sit in the snow while you strap in. So your pants should be waterproof and your gloves sturdy. If the rental shop has knee pads and padded shorts, take them for the first days.

What do you teach in the first lesson?

On skis and on a snowboard the first lesson is structured the same way: getting to know you and choosing your gear, balance on flat ground, first descents on a gentle slope and a solo descent at the end. Only the exercises within each step differ.
